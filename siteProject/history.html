The History of EDM

Electronic music is not new — indeed, it was invented by Dr. Lee de Forest. The first public account of such an instrument appeared in my former publication, the Electrical Experimenter, of December 1915, when Dr. de Forest wrote an illustrated article for me entitled, “Audion Bulbs as Producers of Pure Musical Tones.” - Hugo Gernsback

The Past of Electronic Music

Different people will consider the start of electronic music to be at many different places. Some consider it to have begun around the 60s, when electronic elements had begun being used in mainstream songs. Others will go back to the late 1800s when electronics were first being experiemented with in the music scene. Neither are wrong, as it depends on what you define electronic music as. But if you want to know the full history, you're at the right place.

Late 1800s - 1930s

Electronic music started out as a concept more than a public tool. Early instruments included the Telharmonium which slowly gained public interest over a couple of years. Critics at the time saw promise in these new instruments. Most music made with electronics at the time would be experimental, using things such as the vacuum tube and other electronic instruments. These instruments allowed for a wider range of sounds never heard before.

1940s to 1950s

By the mid 1930s, audio tape recorders had been invented and were constantly being improved upon, even becoming stereo sound (different audio per ear). Beyond that, electronic and acoustic instruments began to be used alongside each other in music. Different countries all made independent advancements towards electronics within music, but they were all headed in the same direction. At this time, the first computer to ever play music was made, being the CSIRAC, the 5th "stored-program" computer globally. Various albums and tracks continued to be made and rise in popularity, utilising the power of electronics.

Early 1960s

With the 60s came the rise of the Synthesizer, ane electronic piano capable of producing sounds never heard before. This instrument began to be used mainstream media like the Doctor Who theme. More research and experimentation was done by various composers, leading to the start of different electronic "styles". Synthesizers begun being used in live performances, along with various forms of electronic drums. As previously, more experiemental music was being created with methods such as tape-based composition.

Late 1960s - Early 1980s

Around this time, familiar names begun to rise, using electronic instruments in their music. Names such as the Beach Boys and the Beatles were among the most popular, while the Doors and the Monkees were among the first to do so. Electronics became heavily incorporated into other mainstream genres, creating eras such as "electronic rock" and "electronica". Synthesizers continued to become more mainstream and more popular, as more companies made varying kinds with varying success.

Various electronic subgenres began to emerge, such as Dub music, Synth-pop, and Chiptune. Also around this time, a method known as sampling (or borrowing parts from other songs) began to be normalised. Advancements in technology allowed for more variety with electronic instruments, allowing for more types of synthesizers, and a combined interface called a MIDI. Electronic music had become more accessible to the people and had already caught on to the masses.

Late 1980s - 1990s

While EDM is known as "dance music", the dance aspect of electronic music didn't actually emerge until around this time. Many well-known subgenres emerged from this time, includng Dance-Pop, House, Techno, Trance, Drum and Bass, Hardstyle, Glitch, and more. By this point, electronic music had already become mainstream and was incorporated into the majority of all music genres, and electronic music itself had become so diverse that new styles were being made yearly, if not more.

2000s - Today

With the rise of computers, the internet, and many more technological advances, electronic music has never been as accessible or diverse. Digital Audio Workspaces (or DAWs) become common use for music producers, allowing for a wide variety of music to be made almost anywhere. Many genres were made in this time, such as Dubstep and its various sub-subgenres, Nightcore, Phonk, Synthwave, Vocaloid, Future Bass, Digicore, and so much more. Electronic music has defined itself as the most diverse genre of music ever, and is still being experimented with and created to this very day.
